# Proven — Condiments & Umami

The flavour boosters I keep. Other options considered are in
`../candidates/condiments-and-umami.md`.

**Dominant criterion: umami + versatility.** These earn their place on flavour, not
nutrition — used by the spoonful, so low nutrient density isn't a mark against them
(sodium runs high; reach for reduced-salt where it exists).

| Rank | Ingredient | Why here | Versatility | Prep effort | Availability | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soy sauce | The universal umami-and-salt base — most versatile and most available bottle in the category. | 🟢 stir-fries, marinades, dressings, eggs, soups | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 small amounts; high sodium |
| 2 | Tomato purée (paste) | Concentrated umami-per-spoon that bodies up almost any Western or Indian pot. | 🟢 pasta, stews, curries, chilli, sauce bases | 🟢 spoon in, cook out 1 min | 🟢 | 🟡 lycopene, potassium, some vit C |
| 3 | Stock / bouillon paste | Instant savoury foundation under any soup, grain or braise; paste beats cubes for taste. | 🟢 soups, risotto, cooking grains, braises | 🟢 spoon or crumble + dissolve | 🟢 | 🔴 mostly salt; yeast types add B-vits |
| 4 | Miso | The deepest fermented umami here plus genuine nutrition; only availability keeps it off the podium. | 🟢 soup, dressings, glazes, marinades | 🟢 spoon + stir off the boil | 🟡 UK world-food aisle / Asian shops | 🟡 protein, vit K, minerals; high sodium |
| 5 | Fish sauce | One of the biggest umami bombs — essential to SE-Asian food and a secret-weapon depth in Western braises. | 🟡 SE-Asian + secret-weapon depth | 🟢 just splash | 🟡 UK 🟡, 🟢 SE Asia | 🔴 small amounts; trace iodine; high sodium |

**Notes:**
- Sodium runs high — soy, fish sauce, miso and stock especially; reduced-salt soy and low-salt stock/miso swap in 1:1.
- Soy variants: tamari is gluten-free and rounder; reduced-salt for everyday pouring.
- Buy stock as paste/concentrate (Marigold, Knorr) over cubes — cleaner dissolve, better taste.
- Miso: stir in off the boil; only raw/unpasteurised (fridge-aisle) miso has live cultures. Keeps for months in the fridge.
- Tomato purée: a quick fry-off in oil deepens it before liquid goes in.
