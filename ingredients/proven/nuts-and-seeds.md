# Proven — Nuts & Seeds

The everyday nuts and seeds. Other options considered are in
`../candidates/nuts-and-seeds.md`.

**Dominant criterion: nutrition (healthy fats, protein, minerals) + no-cook
convenience.** Taste is high across the board, so it barely separates them.

| Rank | Ingredient | Why here | Versatility | Prep effort | Availability | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Peanut butter | Among the highest protein per spoon, ubiquitous and no-cook — the obvious staple. | 🟢 toast, satay sauce, oats, dip, stir-in | 🟢 spoon/spread, no cook | 🟢 | 🟢 protein (~25g/100g), niacin, vit E, magnesium, MUFA |
| 2 | Almonds | The vitamin-E champion of nuts, plus calcium and fibre; snack or chop in. | 🟡 snack, chopped on salads/oats/yoghurt | 🟢 grab a handful, no cook | 🟢 | 🟢 vit E, magnesium, calcium, fibre, protein |
| 3 | Pumpkin seeds | The most mineral-dense sprinkle here — magnesium and zinc standouts, no cook. | 🟢 sprinkle on salads, soups, oats, yoghurt | 🟢 sprinkle, no cook | 🟢 | 🟢 magnesium, zinc (~7.5mg/100g), iron, protein |

**Notes:**
- Buy natural peanut butter (just peanuts, maybe salt) over the sugared/palm-oil kind.
- Nut butters and oily seeds go rancid — buy small, keep cool.
- Tahini stays in candidates: its main use is hummus, which is proven (see `other-proteins.md`); on its own it's a sesame paste loosened with water + lemon for dressings.
- Peanut allergy: almonds or pumpkin seeds cover the same role.
