# Acids

The brightening lever from *Salt Fat Acid Heat* — a squeeze or splash at the end wakes up almost any pot or pan; most of these are zero-effort and several keep forever.

| Rank | Ingredient | Why here | Versatility | Prep effort | Availability | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fresh lemon | Brightest all-rounder, real vitamin C, and a whole fruit keeps 3–4 weeks in the fridge. | 🟢 dressings, fish, greens, soups, water | 🟢 halve + squeeze | 🟢 | 🟢 vit C (~22mg of usable juice; ~34mg whole fruit), flavonoids |
| 2 | Fresh lime | The lemon of the East/Latin kitchen; non-negotiable for Thai and taco-style food. | 🟢 SE-Asian, Mexican, curries | 🟢 halve + squeeze | 🟢 | 🟢 vit C (about half a lemon's, ~20mg/fruit), flavonoids |
| 3 | Rice vinegar | Mild and slightly sweet so it brightens without dominating; keeps indefinitely. | 🟢 stir-fry finish, quick pickles, dressings | 🟢 just pour | 🟡 most UK + Asian shops | 🔴 negligible (acetic acid) |
| 4 | Red wine vinegar | The default European table acid — sharp, savoury, endlessly useful, immortal in the cupboard. | 🟢 vinaigrettes, deglaze, marinades, lentils | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 negligible; trace polyphenols |
| 5 | Apple cider vinegar | Fruity-sharp workhorse with mild blood-sugar upside, though its assertive flavour limits delicate dishes. | 🟡 slaws, braises, dressings | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 negligible; trace potassium |
| 6 | White wine vinegar | Cleaner and more neutral than red — the safe choice when you don't want colour or sweetness. | 🟢 dressings, pan sauces, poaching | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 negligible |
| 7 | Balsamic vinegar | Sweet depth that reduces to a glaze in the pan, but the sugar narrows its savoury range. | 🟡 salads, tomatoes, quick glaze | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 negligible; sugars + polyphenols |
| 8 | Bottled lemon/lime juice | The no-knife, no-waste citrus standby; a real finishing acid when fresh fruit isn't around, though flavour and vitamin C are duller. | 🟡 cooking backup | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🟡 some vit C, less than fresh |
| 9 | Distilled white vinegar | Cheapest and sharpest acid going, but too harsh and one-note for most finishing. | 🔴 pickling, cleaning | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 none |
| 10 | Malt vinegar | A UK staple with real character but a narrow remit, and scarce outside Britain. | 🟡 chips, pickles, slaws | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 UK, 🟡 elsewhere | 🔴 negligible |
| 11 | Tamarind concentrate | Unique sweet-sour fruit acid that earns its place in specific dishes; buy paste/concentrate, not the soak-it block. | 🟡 pad thai, curries, chutney | 🟢 spoon + dissolve | 🟡 Asian aisle / shops | 🟡 potassium, magnesium, B vits |
| 12 | Sumac | Lemony acid you can sprinkle dry over salads, eggs and meats — handy but only ever a garnish. | 🟡 finishing dust, dressings | 🟢 sprinkle | 🟡 spice aisle / Middle-Eastern | 🟡 antioxidant-rich but only a sprinkle's worth |

**Notes:**
- Acids are flavour tools, not nutrition sources — fresh lemon and lime are the only real nutrient contributors, and lime carries about half the vitamin C of lemon (~20mg vs a lemon's ~22mg usable juice / ~34mg whole fruit), so don't rank vinegars for nutrition.
- Fresh citrus is the one perishable group (weeks in the fridge); every vinegar essentially keeps forever, making them the most Airbnb-friendly pantry buy.
- Rice vinegar, tamarind and sumac are everyday in East/SE Asian and Middle-Eastern shops but only 🟡 in a default UK supermarket; malt vinegar is the reverse — 🟢 in Britain, scarce elsewhere.
- Honourable mentions: **sherry vinegar** (superb but specialty/pricey), **pomegranate molasses** (🔴, lovely sweet-tart drizzle), **amchur / dried mango powder** (🔴, a dry sumac-style souring dust), and **caper/pickle brine** (free acid you already have in the jar).
