# Nutrition Powerhouses — candidates

Ranked add-on foods for a big nutrition or completeness hit — none promoted yet; review and move picks into `../proven/`.

| Rank | Ingredient | Why here | Versatility | Prep effort | Availability | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ground flaxseed (milled) | Top nutrient-per-spoon add-on — huge plant omega-3 plus lignans, and it just stirs in. | 🟢 oats, yoghurt, soups, stir-in | 🟢 stir in (buy pre-milled) | 🟢 | 🟢 ALA omega-3, lignans, soluble fibre |
| 2 | Chia seeds | Same omega-3 league as flax with even more fibre and no grinding, and it gels into a no-cook pudding. | 🟢 pudding, oats, yoghurt, jam | 🟢 stir in or soak (passive) | 🟢 | 🟢 ALA omega-3, fibre, calcium |
| 3 | Nutritional yeast (fortified) | The easiest reliable plant B12 hit on a plant-leaning diet — but only the **fortified** packs; plain/"unfortified" nooch has none. | 🟢 pasta, popcorn, eggs, soups | 🟢 sprinkle, no cook | 🟡 health aisle / H&B | 🟢 B12 + B-vitamins (fortified only; ~4–24 µg B12 per 2 tbsp), protein (small amounts as used) |
| 4 | Hemp hearts | Near-complete protein plus omega-3/6 and a lot of magnesium with zero prep; only health-shop stocking holds it back. | 🟢 yoghurt, salads, oats, stir-in | 🟢 sprinkle, no cook | 🟡 health shops, Grape Tree/H&B | 🟢 protein (~31g/100g, all 9 amino acids), omega-3/6, magnesium, zinc |
| 5 | Protein powder (whey/pea/soy) | A 20–25g protein top-up in one shake — unbeatable for the protein gap, thin on micronutrients. | 🟡 shakes, oats, yoghurt | 🟢 shake/stir, no cook | 🟢 whey/pea widely sold | 🟢 protein (~20–25g/scoop); whey/soy complete, leucine |
| 6 | Wheat germ | Genuinely nutrient-dense whole-food stir-in (vit E, folate, zinc, thiamin); its only real knock is poor availability and fast rancidity. | 🟡 oats, yoghurt, stir-in | 🟢 stir in (refrigerate after opening) | 🔴 health shops; rare in Asia | 🟢 vitamin E, folate, magnesium, zinc, thiamin |
| 7 | Huel / meal-replacement powder | The most complete and convenient option here, but it's an ultra-processed meal *replacement*, not a whole-food add-on. | 🟡 shake as a meal | 🟢 shake, no cook | 🟡 mostly online / H&B | 🟢 26–27 vitamins/minerals, ~30g protein per 400 kcal meal (Black Edition ~40g), fibre (ultra-processed) |
| 8 | Fortified plant milk | Not dense, but a no-effort base delivering fortified calcium, B12, vit D and (sometimes) iodine — check the label, as organic versions usually aren't fortified. | 🟢 oats, coffee, sauces, drinking | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🟡 fortified Ca/B12/D and sometimes iodine; soy adds protein |
| 9 | Dried seaweed / nori | The simplest *safe* dietary iodine — nori is the lowest-iodine seaweed, so it tops up a hard-to-hit nutrient without the excess risk of kelp/kombu — plus umami, though eaten in tiny amounts. | 🟡 snack, crumble into rice/soup/eggs | 🟢 tear/crumble, no cook | 🟡 sushi aisle; ubiquitous in Asia | 🟡 iodine (modest, ~16–43 µg/g; safer than high-iodine kelp/kombu); otherwise small amounts |
| 10 | Brazil nuts | One nut covers a full day's selenium — a nutrient easy to under-eat in low-soil regions — with zero prep; just cap at 1–2/day (selenium has a low safe ceiling). | 🟢 snack, chop into oats/yoghurt/salads | 🟢 eat as-is / chop, no cook | 🟢 supermarket nut aisle | 🟢 selenium (1 nut ≈ a day's RDA), magnesium, healthy fats (UL: ≤1–2/day) |
| 11 | Cacao / cocoa (unsweetened) | Real magnesium plus great flavour, but the famed flavanols are largely lost in processing (especially Dutch/alkalised) and the iron is poorly absorbed — and you use a spoonful. | 🟢 oats, milk, yoghurt, chilli | 🟢 stir in | 🟢 | 🟡 magnesium, some iron (poorly absorbed), fibre (small amounts; flavanols overstated) |
| 12 | Spirulina | The most overstated "superfood" here — real iron but in tiny doses, an inactive pseudo-B12, strong taste and heavy-metal risk. | 🔴 hard to hide; shakes only | 🟢 stir/shake | 🔴 supplement/health shops | 🟡 iron, protein but only in tiny servings; "B12" is an inactive analogue; heavy-metal/microcystin risk if not third-party tested |

**Notes:**
- Buy flax **pre-milled** — whole flax passes through undigested. Flax and wheat germ both go rancid; keep cool and buy small bags.
- B12 only counts if the pack says "fortified" / "with B12" — plain nutritional yeast isn't a source, and spirulina's pseudo-B12 doesn't count. Fortified nooch is potent: one 2-tbsp serving can clear a day's B12 several times over.
- Iodine gap: lean on nori or fortified plant milk (or iodised salt) if you skip dairy and fish. Nori is the deliberately *safe* pick — kelp/kombu can deliver iodine far in excess.
- Selenium gap: one Brazil nut covers a day, but don't exceed ~2/day — the selenium upper limit (~400 µg) is easy to blow past at ~70–90 µg per nut.
- Availability flags: hemp hearts, wheat germ and spirulina are mostly health-shop / online in the UK; nutritional yeast (Engevita) and nori are now mainstream-ish but not universal (and still rare across much of Asia). Organic plant milks usually aren't fortified — check the label.
- Weakest nutrition claims to discount: spirulina overall; cocoa's flavanols *and* its poorly-absorbed (non-heme) iron specifically.
- Honourable mentions: LSA blend (linseed-sunflower-almond), pumpkin seeds, ground almonds — same no-cook stir-in role.
