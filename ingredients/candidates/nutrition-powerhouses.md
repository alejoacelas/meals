# Nutrition Powerhouses — candidates

Other add-ons considered. The go-tos — ground flaxseed and chia — live in
`../proven/nutrition-powerhouses.md`.

| Rank | Ingredient | Why here | Versatility | Prep effort | Availability | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nutritional yeast (fortified) | The easiest reliable plant B12 hit — but only the **fortified** packs; plain nooch has none. | 🟢 pasta, popcorn, eggs, soups | 🟢 sprinkle, no cook | 🟡 health aisle / H&B | 🟢 B12 + B-vitamins (fortified only; ~4–24 µg B12 per 2 tbsp) |
| 2 | Hemp hearts | Near-complete protein plus omega-3/6 and a lot of magnesium with zero prep; only stocking holds it back. | 🟢 yoghurt, salads, oats, stir-in | 🟢 sprinkle, no cook | 🟡 health shops, Grape Tree/H&B | 🟢 protein (~31g/100g, all 9 amino acids), omega-3/6, magnesium, zinc |
| 3 | Protein powder (whey/pea/soy) | A 20–25g protein top-up in one shake — unbeatable for the protein gap, thin on micronutrients. | 🟡 shakes, oats, yoghurt | 🟢 shake/stir, no cook | 🟢 whey/pea widely sold | 🟢 protein (~20–25g/scoop); whey/soy complete, leucine |
| 4 | Wheat germ | Genuinely nutrient-dense whole-food stir-in; only real knock is poor availability and fast rancidity. | 🟡 oats, yoghurt, stir-in | 🟢 stir in (refrigerate after opening) | 🔴 health shops; rare in Asia | 🟢 vitamin E, folate, magnesium, zinc, thiamin |
| 5 | Huel / meal-replacement powder | The most complete and convenient option here, but an ultra-processed meal *replacement*, not a whole-food add-on. | 🟡 shake as a meal | 🟢 shake, no cook | 🟡 mostly online / H&B | 🟢 26–27 vitamins/minerals, ~30g protein per 400 kcal (ultra-processed) |
| 6 | Fortified plant milk | Not dense, but a no-effort base delivering fortified calcium, B12, vit D and sometimes iodine — check the label. | 🟢 oats, coffee, sauces, drinking | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🟡 fortified Ca/B12/D, sometimes iodine; soy adds protein |
| 7 | Dried seaweed / nori | The simplest *safe* dietary iodine — nori is the lowest-iodine seaweed, so it tops up the nutrient without the kelp/kombu excess risk. | 🟡 snack, crumble into rice/soup/eggs | 🟢 tear/crumble, no cook | 🟡 sushi aisle; ubiquitous in Asia | 🟡 iodine (modest, ~16–43 µg/g; safer than kelp/kombu) |
| 8 | Brazil nuts | One nut covers a full day's selenium with zero prep; cap at 1–2/day (low safe ceiling). | 🟢 snack, chop into oats/yoghurt/salads | 🟢 eat as-is / chop, no cook | 🟢 supermarket nut aisle | 🟢 selenium (1 nut ≈ a day's RDA), magnesium (UL: ≤1–2/day) |
| 9 | Cacao / cocoa (unsweetened) | Real magnesium plus great flavour, but the famed flavanols are largely lost in processing and the iron is poorly absorbed. | 🟢 oats, milk, yoghurt, chilli | 🟢 stir in | 🟢 | 🟡 magnesium, some iron (poorly absorbed); flavanols overstated |
| 10 | Spirulina | The most overstated "superfood" here — real iron but tiny doses, an inactive pseudo-B12, strong taste and heavy-metal risk. | 🔴 hard to hide; shakes only | 🟢 stir/shake | 🔴 supplement/health shops | 🟡 iron, protein only in tiny servings; "B12" inactive; heavy-metal risk if untested |

**Notes:**
- B12 only counts if the pack says "fortified" / "with B12" — plain nutritional yeast isn't a source, and spirulina's pseudo-B12 doesn't count.
- Iodine gap: lean on nori or fortified plant milk (or iodised salt) if you skip dairy and fish. Nori is the deliberately *safe* pick — kelp/kombu can deliver iodine far in excess.
- Selenium gap: one Brazil nut covers a day, but don't exceed ~2/day — the selenium upper limit (~400 µg) is easy to blow past.
- Availability — hemp hearts, wheat germ and spirulina are mostly health-shop/online; nutritional yeast and nori are mainstream-ish but rare across much of Asia. Organic plant milks usually aren't fortified — check the label.
- Weakest claims to discount: spirulina overall; cocoa's flavanols and its poorly-absorbed iron.
- Honourable mentions: LSA blend (linseed-sunflower-almond), pumpkin seeds, ground almonds — same no-cook stir-in role.
