# Condiments & Umami — candidates

Ranked options, none promoted yet — review and move picks into `../proven/`. Ranked on umami punch and versatility, the criterion that decides this category; nearly all are used by the spoonful, so low nutrient density is never a mark against them (sodium is flagged, not disqualifying).

| Rank | Ingredient | Why here | Versatility | Prep effort | Availability | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soy sauce | The universal umami-and-salt base — most versatile and most available bottle in the category. | 🟢 stir-fries, marinades, dressings, eggs, soups | 🟢 just pour | 🟢 | 🔴 small amounts; high sodium |
| 2 | Tomato purée (paste) | Concentrated umami-per-spoon that bodies up almost any Western or Indian pot, with real nutrition for a condiment. | 🟢 pasta, stews, curries, chilli, sauce bases | 🟢 spoon in, cook out 1 min | 🟢 | 🟡 lycopene, potassium, some vit C |
| 3 | Stock / bouillon paste | Instant savoury foundation under any soup, grain or braise; paste beats cubes for taste and texture. | 🟢 soups, risotto, cooking grains, braises | 🟢 spoon or crumble + dissolve | 🟢 | 🔴 mostly salt; yeast types add B-vits |
| 4 | Miso | The deepest fermented umami here plus genuine nutrition; only availability keeps it off the podium. | 🟢 soup, dressings, glazes, marinades, butter | 🟢 spoon + stir off the boil | 🟡 UK world-food aisle / Asian shops | 🟡 protein, vit K, minerals; live cultures only if raw; high sodium |
| 5 | Fish sauce | One of the category's biggest umami bombs — essential to SE-Asian food and a secret-weapon depth in Western braises and dressings. | 🟡 SE-Asian + secret-weapon depth | 🟢 just splash | 🟡 UK 🟡, 🟢 across SE Asia | 🔴 small amounts; trace iodine; high sodium |
| 6 | Oyster sauce | Glossy, MSG-rich umami bomb (natural oyster glutamates plus added MSG) that deepens stir-fries, glazes, noodles and even Western gravies. | 🟢 stir-fries, marinades, glazes, greens, gravies | 🟢 just spoon | 🟡 UK world-food aisle, 🟢 East/SE Asia | 🔴 small amounts; sugar + sodium; not veggie (mushroom version exists) |
| 7 | Gochujang | Sweet-spicy-savoury fermented chilli paste, real fermented umami, now widely stocked and at home in stews, glazes and noodles. | 🟡 stews, marinades, noodles, eggs, dressings | 🟢 spoon + stir | 🟡 UK chains, 🟢 Korea | 🔴 small amounts; sugar + sodium |
| 8 | Worcestershire sauce | A few drops of anchovy-tamarind-molasses umami deepen stews, mince and cheese on toast; a classic background booster. | 🟡 stews, mince, marinades, cheese on toast | 🟢 just splash | 🟢 West, 🟡 Asia | 🔴 negligible; tiny amounts |
| 9 | Mustard (Dijon) | Pungent, tangy emulsifier workhorse — light on umami but a tiny spoon sharpens and binds any dressing, sauce or marinade. | 🟢 dressings, pan sauces, marinades, sandwiches | 🟢 just spoon | 🟢 West, 🟡 Asia | 🔴 small amounts; trace selenium |
| 10 | Harissa | Smoky North-African chilli paste that doubles as heat and some depth across eggs, stews and dressings. | 🟡 stews, eggs, roasting, dressings | 🟢 just spoon | 🟡 common in West, patchier in Asia | 🟡 chilli antioxidants; small amounts |
| 11 | Hot sauce (Sriracha / Tabasco) | The near-universal finisher — adds heat and tang to almost any plate, though it's a topper more than a cooking base and brings little umami. | 🟢 finisher on almost anything | 🟢 just shake | 🟢 | 🔴 negligible; capsaicin, trace vit C |
| 12 | Ketchup | Sweet-tangy-umami that everyone has, but the sugar load and narrow cooking role keep it low. | 🟡 dipping, quick sauces, marinades | 🟢 just squeeze | 🟢 | 🔴 sugar-heavy; some lycopene |
| 13 | Yeast extract (Marmite) | Near-pure glutamate punch — one of the most intense umami hits in the category; what keeps it low is narrow, polarizing, very-British use and reach, not weak flavour. | 🔴 toast, stock/gravy booster, stew depth | 🟢 tiny scrape / dissolve | 🟢 UK/ANZ, 🔴 elsewhere | 🟢 B-vitamins, added B12 + folate |

**Notes:**
- Sodium runs high across the board — soy, fish sauce, oyster sauce, miso, stock and Marmite especially; reduced-salt soy and low-salt stock/miso swap in 1:1.
- Soy variants: tamari is gluten-free and rounder; light vs dark (dark is sweeter, mainly for colour); reduced-salt for everyday pouring.
- Availability flags: fish sauce, oyster sauce, miso and gochujang are everyday in their home regions but only 🟡 in a default UK/Western shop (world-food aisle); harissa is the reverse — common in the West, patchier in Asia; Marmite is a UK/ANZ thing, scarce across most of the West and Asia.
- Buy stock as paste/concentrate (Marigold, Knorr) over cubes — cleaner dissolve, better taste; yeast-based ones add B-vitamins.
- Miso probiotics are oversold: only raw/unpasteurized (fridge-aisle) miso has live cultures, and they die above ~46°C, so "stir in off the boil" preserves them only for raw miso — most shelf-stable miso is pasteurized with none. Either way it keeps for months in the fridge.
- Tomato purée: a quick fry-off in oil deepens it before liquid goes in.
- Oyster sauce isn't vegetarian; mushroom ("vegetarian oyster") versions give most of the same glossy umami.
- Honourable mentions: hoisin, nutritional yeast (vegan cheesy umami; B12 only in fortified brands), MSG (purest umami, a kitchen enhancer more than a table condiment), anchovy paste, doubanjiang/chilli-bean paste, dashi/kombu.
