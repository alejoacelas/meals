## Sweet-&-Sour Braised Cabbage with Chickpeas

**Equipment:** one pot / knife
**Serves:** 1–2

Cabbage braised slow until it collapses sweet and glossy, then lifted bright with red-wine vinegar. Tangy, moreish, made in one pot — and like all good braises, better the next day.

### Core recipe — proven ingredients only
**Ingredients**
- ¼ cabbage, shredded (a few big handfuls)
- 1 carrot, grated or thinly sliced
- 1 onion, sliced
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced
- 1 can chickpeas, drained
- 1–2 tbsp red wine vinegar
- A good pinch of chilli flakes
- Extra-virgin olive oil
- Fine sea salt, black pepper

**Method**
1. Warm a generous glug of olive oil in the pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot and a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then, until soft and sweet — 6–8 min. Add the garlic and chilli flakes and stir until fragrant, ~1 min.
2. Pile in the cabbage with a splash of water and a good pinch of salt. Cover and let it wilt down, 3–4 min, then stir. Lower the heat and braise, uncovered, stirring occasionally and adding a splash more water if it sticks, until the cabbage is silky, glossy and sweet — 20–25 min.
3. Stir in the chickpeas and warm through, ~3 min, mashing a few against the side of the pot to help them carry the sauce.
4. Off the heat, stir in the red wine vinegar and plenty of black pepper. Taste: it should be sweet, tangy and savoury all at once — add more vinegar or salt to balance. Serve, or cool and reheat tomorrow when it's even better.

### Level it up (optional — beyond the kit, widely available)
- **Smoky warmth (+ smoked paprika, not in kit):** a teaspoon with the garlic adds depth that plays against the sweet-sour.
- **Toasty seeds (+ caraway or fennel seeds, not in kit):** a pinch toasted with the onion gives an aromatic, almost sauerkraut-like lift.
- **More sweet-sour (+ grated apple, not in kit):** a small grated apple stirred in with the cabbage melts down and deepens the sweet edge; a drizzle of honey (not in kit) at the end sharpens the balance.
- **Richer finish (+ butter, not in kit; + dill, not in kit):** a knob of butter stirred in off the heat makes it glossy and round, and a scatter of dill brings it fresh and bright.

**Why it scores well:** Dominant criterion is **availability** — a wedge of cabbage, a carrot, an onion and a tin of chickpeas are everywhere and keep for weeks, cooked in one pot with a knife. Chickpeas and cabbage carry real protein, fibre and vitamin C/K (nutrition 🟢); slow braising turns the cheapest veg genuinely tasty (taste 🟢). Effort is 🟡 only for the unhurried braise — it's one continuous, low-attention session and the batch reheats beautifully.
