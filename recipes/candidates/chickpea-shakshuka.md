Eggs poached into a cumin-spiced tomato-and-chickpea sauce — a complete,
restaurant-feeling, protein-rich dinner from a single pan. The flagship shakshuka
that every other variant builds on.

### Core recipe — proven ingredients only
**Ingredients**
- 4 eggs
- 1 can chickpeas, drained
- 1 can chopped tomatoes (~400g)
- 1 bell pepper, sliced
- 1 onion, diced
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- chilli flakes, to taste
- olive oil, salt, black pepper

**Method**
1. Warm a good glug of olive oil in the pan; soften the onion and pepper over
   medium heat, 6–8 min, until sweet and slumping.
2. Stir in the garlic, cumin and chilli flakes; cook 1 min until fragrant.
3. Pour in the tomatoes and chickpeas. Season with salt and pepper and simmer,
   stirring now and then, 8–10 min, until thick enough to hold a spoon-trail.
4. Make four wells in the sauce and crack an egg into each. Cover the pan and
   cook gently, 6–8 min, until the whites are set but the yolks still soft.
5. Finish with black pepper and another pinch of chilli. Serve from the pan.

### Level it up (optional — beyond the kit, widely available)
- **Salty creaminess (+ feta, not in kit):** crumble over the eggs before they
  finish — it softens into salty pockets against the spiced sauce.
- **Smokier, deeper sauce (+ smoked paprika, not in kit):** a teaspoon alongside
  the cumin pushes it toward the classic; (+ harissa, not in kit) stirred in adds
  real heat.
- **Fresh finish (+ fresh coriander or parsley, not in kit):** scatter over at the
  end for a herb lift; a handful of spinach (in kit) wilted into the sauce first
  bumps the greens.
- **Something to mop with (+ crusty bread or pita, not in kit):** for scooping up
  sauce and runny yolk — turns it into a fuller, restaurant-like plate.

**Why it scores well:** Two proteins (eggs + chickpeas) plus fibre and vit-C-rich
pepper and tomato make it genuinely nutrient-dense; it's all pantry-and-shelf
staples, one pan, one continuous session — and it tastes like something you'd
order out, which is why taste is the criterion it wins on.
